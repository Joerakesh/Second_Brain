# Why You're Always Tired — Sleep, Energy & Focus

## Core takeaway

**Productivity is not just about how many hours you sleep or how hard you work. It also depends on your body clock, sleep quality, mental habits, and when you choose to do demanding work.**

The goal is to work _with your biology_, rather than forcing yourself into a routine that doesn't suit you.

## Key takeaways

### 1. Keep your bed associated with sleep

- Avoid working, scrolling, checking messages, or planning tomorrow while lying in bed.
    
- Repeated habits can create associations between your bed and mental stimulation.
    
- If you find yourself worrying or planning, write your thoughts down somewhere else and return to bed when sleepy.
    

**Remember:** Bed is for rest, not an extension of your workspace.

### 2. Understand your body clock

- People differ in their natural sleep and wake preferences, known as their **chronotype**.
    
- Some people naturally feel more alert earlier, while others peak later.
    
- A productive routine should account for your natural rhythm instead of blindly copying someone else's schedule.
    

**Remember:** Don't confuse someone else's ideal routine with your own ideal routine.

### 3. Protect sleep quality

- Getting enough time in bed does not automatically mean you feel refreshed.
    
- Sleep continuity, regularity, and the different stages of sleep all matter.
    
- Deep sleep is involved in physical restoration and brain processes, including memory and aspects of emotional regulation.
    

**Remember:** Aim for sufficient, consistent, restorative sleep—not just a number on the clock.

### 4. Identify your peak-focus window

- Notice when your concentration and energy are naturally strongest.
    
- Reserve your best focus period for difficult work, such as studying, programming, problem-solving, or learning complex concepts.
    
- Use lower-energy periods for routine tasks, administration, and lighter work.
    

**Remember:** Schedule important work around your energy, not just your available time.

### 5. Create a shutdown ritual

- Develop a consistent cue that signals the end of work.
    
- This might be a particular playlist, preparing tomorrow's task list, or a short relaxation routine.
    
- A clear stopping point can help separate work from rest.
    

**Remember:** Give your brain a transition between working and sleeping.

### 6. Make phone use intentional

- Keep your phone away from your bed.
    
- Create a designated place to leave it before your nighttime routine.
    
- Reduce the temptation to scroll or check notifications when you should be winding down.
    

**Remember:** Make the habit you want easier and the distracting habit harder.

## Practical action plan

### Start with these five habits

- Put my phone away before getting into bed.
    
- Maintain a reasonably consistent sleep and wake schedule.
    
- Track my energy and concentration for seven days.
    
- Identify my strongest two-to-three-hour focus window.
    
- Create a simple end-of-work routine before sleeping.

|---|---|
|Bed association|Keep bed primarily for sleep|
|Chronotype|Work with your natural body clock|
|Sleep quality|Duration alone doesn't tell the whole story|
|Peak-focus window|Do difficult work when most alert|

## Final lesson

**Don't optimize only for waking up early. Optimize for feeling rested, thinking clearly, and using your best hours well.**

The objective is sustainable energy and consistent performance, not simply squeezing more work into every day.
